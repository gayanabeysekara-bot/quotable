# True-Story Shorts (60-second mechanism stories)

A format for high-retention vertical Shorts: one little-known, true mechanism told as a tiny story with a hero, a doubter and a satisfying reveal. Benchmarked on a channel whose 29 Shorts in this format averaged ~9.5M views each (Oct–Nov 2026). The character and twist drive views, not the art style: generic "how X works" explainers in the same look stalled under 400K.

## 1. Pick the topic
- One true, surprising mechanism per Short: an odd object, job, rule, invention or experiment with a clear "why it works".
- It must have a hero who did something that looked foolish and turned out right.
- Find at least one credible source. Mark anything legendary or rumoured as such, or drop it.
- Fit the channel's audience. For a business or leadership channel, use decisions, incentives, systems, negotiation, psychology at work, operations and AI stories, not random trivia.
- Reject topics with no character, no doubter or no twist.

## 2. Title (4–8 words)
- Pattern: How / Why / The + odd object or outcome. Examples of the shape: "Why Sailors Carried Storm Oil", "The Job Nobody Wants in Japan".
- No emoji, no hashtag stuffing, no "What if / Would you".
- Write 3 options and pick the most curious yet honest one.

## 3. Script (180–240 words, 55–70 seconds)
Follow these beats in order:
1. **Hook (line 1, 0–3s):** the absurd action in one plain sentence, with no date, place intro or greeting. E.g. "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth and told the emperor: if this ever falls out, run."
2. **Mockery:** someone laughs at or doubts the hero.
3. **Stakes plus a concrete number** (every 39 feet, 3 years, last 100 dollars).
4. **It gets worse:** pressure rises or the doubter gets cockier.
5. **The reveal:** one "You see," explanation, ideally an everyday analogy (blowing on soup).
6. **Payoff:** a visual proof on the last line. No moral and no call to action, so the end loops back to the start.

Style: conversational, energetic and short, with at most one proper name. Read it aloud and cut anything that sounds like a lecture.

## 4. Visual plan
- 1080x1920, 30fps.
- Make original visuals only, with consistent simple characters in richly lit sets (AI image or video tools are fine if their output is original). Never reuse films, other creators' comics or another channel's character designs.
- Change the shot every 2–4 seconds using push-ins, close-ups and reaction shots.
- Frame 1 must show the hook action, because the first seconds act as the thumbnail.
- No logo sting, end screen or subscribe card.
- Write a shot list: timestamp, line, shot, motion.

## 5. Audio and captions
- One clear narrator: the creator's own voice or an AI voice they hold the rights to.
- Licensed or original music kept under the voice.
- Burned-in captions one or two words at a time, bold white with a dark outline, lower-centre, timed word by word.

## 6. Publish
- Keep the description short (title plus a source line is fine).
- Turn on multi-language audio or auto-dubbing where available.
- Once the format works, post every one to two days. A posting hour matters less than consistency.
- Only publish with the account owner's approval.

## 7. Quality gate before publishing
- [ ] Hook in the first sentence and the first frame
- [ ] Hero, doubter, number, "You see" reveal and visual payoff are all present
- [ ] 180–240 words, 55–70s
- [ ] Facts checked against a source; legends labelled
- [ ] All visuals, voice and music original or licensed
- [ ] Title 4–8 words, honest, no emoji

## 8. Measure
Track views at 24 hours and 7 days, average view duration or percentage viewed, and comment tone (delighted versus confused). Make more of whatever topic type wins, and drop generic explainers.

## Deliverable per Short
Title options, the final script with beat labels, the shot list, caption and music notes, the source(s), and the quality-gate result.
